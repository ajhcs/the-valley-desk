# GL09: Broad regional discovery and coverage plan

EX33 authorizes implementation with Terra and independent Sol review, and deployment of the readiness-qualified existing-site candidate. The methods and thresholds below remain proposed until their package is executed; earlier planning-only language describes EX32 and does not revoke that later authorization. No deployment has occurred.

**Status: proposed, plan only.** The service is internal-first and intended to be publicly accessible. It should help readers find health technology, biomedical informatics, and medical AI work across all nine DVRPC counties, including obscure and non-journal work and repositories maintained by individuals or small labs. A useful entry may consist of a title or name, a short supported description, links, and a dated affiliation or geographic association. Complete biographies, papers, institutional ownership, star counts, news coverage, or an inventor profile are not prerequisites. Missing fields remain missing and visible where they matter. Do not label the public service as a pilot.

This plan describes work to perform, not acquired sources, enrolled records, release approval, a schedule, or a claim of regional completeness. The existing EX31 materials are development-exposed historical evidence and do not become independent labels or a coverage benchmark merely by inclusion here. GL02 maintains the inventory; GL03 records acquisition outcomes; GL04 decides individual admissions and projections; GL05–GL07 implement findable, understandable presentation; GL08 operates corrections; GL10 independently checks the reader experience; GL11 reviews release evidence.

## Start with a living inventory, not a fixed list of prestigious institutions

Create a versioned, reviewable seed inventory covering **Bucks, Chester, Delaware, Montgomery and Philadelphia counties in Pennsylvania; Burlington, Camden, Gloucester and Mercer counties in New Jersey**. For each, record known universities, colleges, hospitals, research institutes, health systems, civic or community research organizations, relevant labs and initiatives, and promising public project or person-account leads. Discover additional institutions and labs while exploring; a seed can have no immediately eligible work. Record the source and observation date for each institutional location and separately for any campus spanning or near a county boundary. An institution's mailing address alone does not establish the location of every campus, author, repository, or contribution.

Use several independent entry routes for each county's bounded exploration, wherever public evidence exists:

1. **Institution and campus:** research, department, lab, project, technology-transfer, library and campus pages; follow named projects and references beyond the institution's headline news.
2. **Lab or project:** project pages, documentation, software releases, datasets, research outputs and citations; search by topic and method as well as institutional name.
3. **Personal and organization accounts:** inspect public repository metadata and project-specific evidence for both user and organization owners; a personal account is eligible on the same evidence standard as an organization account. Do not assume a personal account belongs to a named author, or that an organization account is the institution, from name similarity, location strings, or a paper affiliation.
4. **Works and non-journal outputs:** publications, author-posted preprints, proceedings, software, documented datasets, project documentation and other substantiated technical work. Track the exact version and date; a software-only project may be valuable without a paper.
5. **Community and forum leads:** public discussion, issue threads, community directories and other accessible conversations can generate leads. Treat a forum claim as a lead unless independently supported for the specific public fact displayed; avoid collecting contacts or unnecessary discussion text.

### Route families and quiet repositories

The manifest records actual terms and sources chosen for each batch. The following are concrete route families, not a claim that any query is exhaustive:

| Discovery entry and query family | Evidence to examine | Recorded outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| County/municipality plus university, hospital, lab, biomedical computing, health informatics, clinical imaging or medical AI; institutional directories and campus maps | Named institution or unit, campus/county source, project or lab links and dated observations | Add verified seed and project leads; leave a county or unit association unresolved if sources do not establish it. |
| Named institution, lab or investigator plus software, code, tool, dataset, GitHub, preprint or project; follow institutional software and documentation links | Public project description, maintained lab/project page, linked repository and any explicit relationship or dated affiliation | Add a linked lead or a standalone eligible work; retain relationship direction and evidence rather than matching on name alone. |
| Verified lab, organization or User account and its public repositories | Account identity and separately supported institutional or lab relationship; complete bounded page sequence, repository metadata and relevant project/documentation links | Inventory all visible public repositories, retain page-completion state, and classify each as reviewed eligible, ineligible, duplicate/mirror, unresolved, blocked or pending. |
| Disease, clinical workflow, imaging modality, biomedical dataset or informatics method plus software, code, preprint, proceedings or open research | Project-specific subject, usable link, version/date, and any supported institution or county association | Record a lead even when a project is small, old, archived or has no publication evidence; inspect follow-up leads in both citation directions. |
| Community directory, issue/discussion, forum or a project’s references and software links | The cited project and independent support for public claims; exact route and direction | Keep unsupported claims as leads, follow relevant links within the frozen bounds, and record blocked or unresolved results. |

For each verified lab, organization or User account chosen for the declared exploration, enumerate **all visible public repositories** through bounded pagination; do not filter to recently pushed or starred projects. Record the account, verification of any claimed relationship, pages or continuation state, observation date, counts returned and pending items. Follow relevant documentation, project and software links to test health-tech relevance and supported regional association. The association may come from a dated institutional or lab project source; a paper is not required. A cap, continuation, refusal or failed page leaves enumeration **incomplete** and the unreviewed repositories pending for a later frozen batch. It cannot be reported as a completed account audit.

For this plan, **paperless** means a listing is qualified without publication evidence; it does not assert that no publication exists. **Low visibility** means a lead was discovered through account enumeration, a project page or a community route, rather than a claim that no news coverage exists. Retain the actual route, avoid treating these operational labels as intrinsic facts about a creator, and apply the same source standards to all account types.

Use a curated frontier of candidate URLs and stable identifiers, the entry route, seed and follow-up reason. Follow both **repository-to-publication** citations and **publication-to-repository** code-availability links as distinct directed observations; also follow bibliography in either direction as a lead, without turning a citation or a name match into proof of collaboration or implementation. Support useful standalone repositories and standalone works when their factual basis is sufficient. Check exact work versions, aliases and immutable identifiers where available. A dated publication affiliation is evidence of that affiliation at publication, not current employment, physical worksite or code authorship. Preserve uncertain matches as unresolved rather than merging them.

For each candidate, record whether it is the original project, a fork, mirror, archived or superseded repository, or whether that relationship remains unknown. Preserve source-reported status and relevant links; prefer a substantiated canonical project for a primary listing, while retaining a fork or historical project when its distinct contribution or the evidence warrants it. Avoid inflating coverage by treating a mirror as an independent project, and do not silently substitute a successor for a cited historical version.

## Freeze a bounded exploration before collection

Before each acquisition batch, GL02 publishes a small exploration manifest: scope and date; nine county seed snapshots; inclusion and exclusion rules; search routes and terms; frontier ordering; time and request ceilings; pagination and stop conditions; review assignments; proposed measures and targets; and a revision identifier. Freeze it before the first request, so later discoveries and deviations are separately dated. New leads can be added to the next frontier without rewriting the initial denominator. Use official provider documentation and source-specific access and reuse terms when selecting methods; the existing [source basis](../metadata-link-pilot-sources.md) covers only the current narrow provider uses and does not authorize a new provider, bulk collection, or copying richer content. This plan assumes no particular API quota, endpoint, paid access, or permission beyond what is independently verified for the chosen method.

Initial **proposed**, reviewable targets for the first batch:

- Start a seed inventory and a county exploration record for all nine counties. For each, attempt at least one institution/campus route and one alternate route from the list above where a usable lead exists. A missing or blocked route is recorded as such, not manufactured into a success.
- Review a bounded discovery frontier of up to **10 leads per county** and up to **90 leads overall** in the first batch, with a proposed **30 minutes per county** of active discovery. Stop at the earlier limit, exhaustion of documented leads, provider refusal, or a safety/access guard; later expansion requires a new reviewed manifest. These first-batch caps are not launch targets, requirements to produce ten eligible records, or promises of regional recall. Preserve all unreviewed leads and incomplete account pages as pending; repeat separately frozen batches until the launch-specific scoped breadth criteria are met or remaining gaps are reviewed and disclosed.
- Before freezing a second batch, review outcomes and open at least one documented follow-up path for each county with no supported eligible record or only one type of entry route. If no defensible route is currently available, record a reasoned defer, owner role, revisit trigger, and visible coverage gap. No county has a mandatory launch record count.
- Before tuning launch discovery or search, freeze a launch-specific **explored-account inventory** and a separate curated **known-project miss-check set**, including small labs and user-owned projects where available. Record source of awareness, selection criteria and exact versions. An independent reviewer checks known eligible misses within the declared explored sources, confirms repairs against their evidence, and records ineligible, uncertain and untestable cases separately. This is a scoped coverage diagnostic, not an independent reference benchmark or a representative sample.

The existing trusted operator or orchestrating engineer may set and freeze these proposed targets **before** collection within existing authority, using independent Sol review of Terra outputs. No hiring, two human labelers, or new source enrollment is a prerequisite for an ordinary factual index. A proposed target cannot silently become an acceptance rule or be weakened after seeing results; record deviations, rationale, impact and a revised version for review; ordinary bounded batches need no blanket new human approval. If the evidence does not support a target, report the gap and the remediation or reviewed defer rather than padding results with weak matches. Before public go-live, identify the accountable maintenance and release operator and an available fallback; one trusted operator with an explicit pause fallback is acceptable. Do not invent a named backup. Obtain the relevant approval for new paid access, release, or a scope extension, and honor existing live-authorization limits.

## Measure what was explored and what remains unknown

Produce a county-by-route ledger from the frozen manifest and later frontier additions. For each county and route, report seeds identified; leads attempted; completed reviews; supported admissions by material type; duplicates, ineligible and unresolved leads; blocked, refused, unavailable or rate-limited sources; and leads left unattempted when a cap is reached. A denominator is the frozen eligible-to-attempt lead set, not all regional projects. For each rate, show numerator, denominator and exclusions; report `unknown` when no defensible denominator exists. A lead is **examined** only when the planned evidence needed to make its decision was actually available, not when a search returned a title.

Keep three different conclusions separate: **source exploration coverage** describes the planned county and route attempts; the **eligible-repository enumeration audit** describes the completed account pages and reviewed eligibility decisions within the frozen explored-account inventory; **region-wide recall** is unknown without a defensible independent regional denominator. An incomplete page sequence cannot pass as a completed enumeration audit. Before launch, compare the frozen miss-check set to the result, independently verify eligible misses in declared explored sources and repair them, or record a specific unresolved blocker for review and disclosure. Never reclassify pending items as ineligible to meet a target.

Track at least:

- **Exploration coverage:** counties and route types attempted out of those planned; document unavailable route types and uncovered or spanning campuses.
- **Known-project misses:** number found, missed, ineligible, unknown or blocked out of the frozen check set, with reasons and a follow-up disposition. A project discovered after the freeze is a later lead, not a retroactive check-set success.
- **Representation bias:** by county and route, record selected or reviewed leads by institution/lab/individual account and project type, including non-journal and software-only work; show how much of the set comes from the largest source cluster and how much is user-owned. These proportions describe the examined frontier, not regional market shares. Document an anti-concentration check against that frontier, identify the largest institution/lab/account cluster and select explicit follow-ups outside famous or dominant clusters, including small labs and personal accounts where evidence supports them. Compare selected and pending leads to detect discarded quieter routes; document unavoidable gaps for review. Do not rank by prestige, stars or news prominence.
- **Missing and uncertain facts:** count candidates and selected records lacking a supported description, dated affiliation, county association, publication, linked repository, owner identity or relationship, as applicable. Missing optional facts do not automatically exclude a useful record. Track misleading or conflicting claims and their resolution separately.
- **Operational outcomes:** search pages and pagination exhausted versus truncated, blocked or unknown; candidate disposition and age; duplicate/alias decisions; observed source dates and stale or retracted work. A returned page is not evidence that the provider's whole corpus was searched.

For each route, keep enough factual search parameters and timestamps to reproduce the bounded question without retaining unnecessary content. Mark a route **saturated only within its stated search, provider, pages and time window** when planned follow-ups yield no new eligible leads; a capped or truncated page, inaccessible source, failure, ambiguous identity, or unsearched provider stays **unknown/incomplete**, not saturated. Preserve conflicting or unverified evidence and stop a blocked request; do not evade refusals or infer absence from them.

## Replan from evidence

At each batch review, compare the ledger with the frozen target, known-project misses and representation patterns. Choose the next smallest work package: explore an underrepresented county or route, resolve an identity or county ambiguity, improve search for a material type, or defer with a reason and revisit trigger. New provider or richer content use returns to GL02–GL04 for a scoped source/terms decision before acquisition. Repeat frozen batches until the agreed launch-specific breadth criteria for planned routes, completed account enumeration and independently checked known misses are met, or the remaining specific gaps are explicitly reviewed and disclosed. Release review evaluates whether remaining gaps are understandable and have an accountable next action; it must not portray a bounded sample as exhaustive coverage.

**Deliverables:** versioned seed inventory and source links; frozen launch account inventory and separate known-project miss-check set; pre-collection manifest; frontier and candidate decisions; per-county/route coverage and unknowns report; frozen known-project check-set results; bias/missingness summary; reviewed changes and next-batch plan; and clear reader-facing coverage limitations. Keep the historical benchmark, receipts and source ledgers unchanged unless a separately authorized process updates them.
