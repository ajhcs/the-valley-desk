# GL08: Lean operations, corrections and release response

**Status: proposed, plan only.** The service is internal-first and publicly accessible when separately approved for release. Its public interface should not call itself a pilot. This document does not authorize recurring jobs, paid access, outreach, source enrollment, deployment, or a schedule. The existing trusted operator or orchestrating engineer can perform ordinary bounded discovery, with Sol, Terra and Astra review as appropriate. Before public go-live, identify the accountable maintenance and release operator and an available fallback; a single trusted operator with an explicit pause fallback is acceptable. Do not invent a named backup. The aim is a small, dependable maintenance loop that can publish useful linked facts without requiring complete biographies or full papers.

## Roles and a proposed weekly rhythm

One trusted operator may hold multiple roles if capacity permits, using Sol, Terra and Astra for independent review as appropriate. Consequential identity, geographic or removal decisions receive an appropriately scoped review; ordinary bounded batches do not require blanket new human approval. Hiring, two human labelers, and new enrollment are not prerequisites for the public factual index.

| Role to assign | Responsibility | Evidence or handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maintainer | Triage intake, run the approved bounded source checks, prepare a change and keep the backlog current. | Batch manifest, outcome log and proposed patch. |
| Editorial/source reviewer | Check relevance, exact attribution, dated affiliation, relationship direction, source basis, ambiguity and reader-facing wording. | Review decision and unresolved questions. |
| Technical/release reviewer | Check generated projection, reader tasks, tests, release target and rollback evidence; coordinate any separately approved release. | Check results, exact revision and release record. |
| Fallback | Cover absence or pause updates and intake processing safely; ensure deferred items have a revisit trigger. | Available fallback or explicit pause procedure, not an invented named backup or public personal-contact requirement. |

When an operator is assigned and recurring work is explicitly authorized, start with a **proposed weekly manual batch**, not a scheduled crawler: approximately **30 minutes** for intake and source-health triage, **60 minutes** for a small bounded discovery/correction batch, and **30 minutes** for review and handoff. Cap a batch at **10 leads or corrections**, whichever the allotted time permits. These are planning assumptions, not service levels; the initial nine-county exploration can span several cycles. Record actual elapsed time, backlog age and unfinished work. If demand exceeds capacity, prioritize correctness and broken or misleading links, document the queue, and propose a changed budget or narrower cadence for review. An urgent harmful or misleading claim can interrupt the routine; there is no need to wait for a weekly batch. No monitoring cadence or automatic collection is active from this plan alone.

The proposed weekly rhythm is a steady-state maintenance assumption, not a waiting period before go-live. GL02–GL09 can complete initial discovery through parallel, separately bounded bootstrap batches within the orchestrator's existing authority. Freeze their aggregate request/time limits independently; do not force the initial build into ten new leads per calendar week or a four-week public trial.

A normal cycle is: review incoming reports and prior deferrals; select a bounded, versioned batch from the frontier; check whether previously cited facts and links still support the displayed record; document outcomes; propose changes; review; run appropriate local checks; and hand off a reviewed revision for the separately authorized release process. Limit source requests and retained fields to the approved batch and stop on refusal, access challenge, rate limit, unexpected sensitive material, or a failed guard. Log the outcome and follow-up rather than bypassing it. Cached/indexed source views and live provider responses should be labeled as such with their observation dates; neither proves current employment or a project's medical effectiveness.

## Intake and disposition

At launch, provide a visible **Report issue / Suggest work** action that links to an existing, confirmed maintained channel or queue. GL00 and GL08 must verify its exact destination, accessibility, ownership and handling before release; do not create a new email address or service by default. A custom anonymous form is optional. Internal reports or pull requests can also feed the queue. A minimal report can provide a record link, the requested correction or lead, and a supporting public link if known. Do not require an account holder to supply a biography, complete publication, personal contact information, or proof of authorship simply to report a problem. A report is a lead, not permission to publish its claims or personal details. Private candidate and review notes never automatically publish; request or retain contact details only when necessary for follow-up. A maintainer records one of: accepted for a scoped change, duplicate, needs evidence, outside scope, deferred with a trigger, rejected with a reason, or urgent hold. Keep the report's access and retention proportional to its content.

For a **new lead**, check the material type and health-related purpose, preserve the exact identity and available facts, find a dated source for any affiliation claim, and decide whether an informative listing can be made without unsupported fields. A user-owned GitHub repository, non-journal output or standalone project can qualify; a missing paper does not require rejection. Account-to-person or account-to-institution relationships require direct support for that relationship. A project/publication link must preserve the actual observed direction. The broader source and identity contracts belong to GL01–GL04, with county coverage tracked in [GL09](coverage.md).

For an **update or correction**, compare the currently displayed claim to the supporting observation and the new evidence. Preserve the original source's native wording and date in its provenance; add a separately attributed correction or a new dated observation where appropriate. Do not silently merge people, infer a current affiliation from an older paper, replace a cited version with a later publication, or turn a broken link into evidence that a project ceased. When evidence conflicts, label the uncertainty or remove the unsupported claim until reviewed. For a **removal**, record the record or field, reason, scope, reviewer and effective revision; assess whether a correction or removal of only the affected fact is sufficient. Honor applicable source-use and privacy constraints through the scoped source review, without imposing a blanket bespoke permission process on every factual link. Preserve appropriate restricted audit evidence without republishing a removed sensitive fact.

## Change and verification path

1. **Prepare:** identify the affected records and links, source-specific basis, dated evidence, expected reader-visible change, dependent records, and rollback target. Decide whether an isolated manual fix suffices or a new source/provider needs a separately reviewed GL02–GL04 change.
2. **Make reviewable:** prepare a small PR or equivalent reviewable patch, include exact source links and observations, explain uncertain facts and any archive/fork/successor decision, and distinguish source-derived metadata from curated regional context. No full source bodies, private data or contacts are needed in the public projection unless a separately approved use requires them.
3. **Review:** check claim-to-source support, account and work identity, provenance, field-level removals, and the reader's ability to distinguish unknown from established. A conflict that cannot be resolved stays labeled or withheld; use appropriately scoped independent review for consequential changes; Sol, Terra or Astra may assist, without a blanket second-human requirement.
4. **Verify locally:** run the checks appropriate to the changed contract, projection and interface. Read back representative records and exercise search, source links, publication/repository relationships, corrections and empty fields. For meaningful changes, check desktop and narrow-screen reader paths and accessible controls. Record exact revision, test outcomes and limitations; a passing local check is not evidence of deployment.
5. **Release separately:** GL11 verifies the reviewed revision and final CI status, approves the release scope, publishes only through an explicitly authorized action, and reads back the actual public result. Record the exact release and evidence. A merge, local pass, or previous release does not itself approve deployment.

Use lightweight, task-specific source-use checks: identify the provider and applicable terms, the specific fields and link to be used, attribution and retention needed, and any access restriction. Escalate only an actual unresolved use or change that requires it. Do not block a basic factual link solely because full-article reuse has not been authorized; do not treat permission for metadata as permission to copy a paper, source code, logo, biography or third-party content.

## Reader tasks and quality signals

Before public release, an assigned reviewer performs short tasks using the actual release candidate. Find a small or personal-account project by a plausible topic or location, then follow its source link. Distinguish dated institutional association, current role and repository ownership. Distinguish a preprint from a final work, and a publication-to-code link from a repository citation. Find an archived or standalone item and interpret a record with missing optional details. Test both a publication and its related repository when that relationship exists, and include at least one narrow-screen or keyboard path. Record task, revision, result and fixes; repeat a failed task after a relevant change. These are usability and correctness checks, **not** independent reference labels, a benchmark or evidence of region-wide recall. GL10 owns the separate quality plan and GL09 reports coverage gaps.

## Stop, correction and rollback

Pause the affected batch or release when a source refuses access, a provider guard fails, a source identity or copyright/use basis is materially uncertain, a displayed claim may be misleading or expose sensitive data, or a test or release readback fails. Preserve the evidence and current state; do not retry a blocked request by evasion. The assigned maintainer records impact, affected records, a safe immediate action and a reviewer. Remove or correct the smallest unsupported public claim when authorized; escalate urgent exposure for a scoped takedown. If a released revision is materially wrong and a narrow correction cannot safely restore service, the release owner uses the previously verified rollback target under the applicable operational authority and checks the public result. Keep rollback readiness and restoration separate from the decision to deploy; a past approval does not authorize a new release or destructive cleanup.

Before GL11 public go-live, the accountable maintenance and release operator reviews open holds, current coverage disclosure, identity/source uncertainties, confirmed access and reuse basis, local and CI results, confirmed maintained report destination, maintenance coverage and available fallback, release and rollback instructions, and the exact revision. A reviewer may accept a documented coverage defer with an owner role and trigger, but may not relabel unknown coverage as complete. If readiness changes, stop, record the new fact, replan the affected GL work and seek the specific remaining release authorization. Do not require a fixed number of records from each county.

## Sustainability and records

Favor the existing stack, bounded manual review, ordinary version control and source links over a new always-on service or paid provider. Measure the actual time and backlog for the first approved cycles before proposing automation, increased collection, external contracts or a paid plan; each needs its own scope and authorization. Keep recurring work small enough for the existing trusted operator, with independent agent review where appropriate and an available fallback or documented pause procedure. The orchestrator freezes proposed batch caps within existing authority; new paid access, public release, and scope extensions need their specific approval, and recurring or live actions remain subject to their existing authorization.

**Deliverables:** accountable maintenance and release identity and available fallback or pause procedure; confirmed maintained reader-report destination; intake and disposition queue; batch manifest and source-health outcomes; reviewed changes and correction/removal log; task-based check results; backlog and coverage-gap review; release/rollback checklist with exact revision and readback; and a short next-cycle decision with proposed time and limits. Keep existing model, source index, benchmark, package ledger and historical receipts unchanged unless their own authorized workflow requires a separately recorded update.
